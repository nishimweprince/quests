# WAFAA Homepage — Design Rationale

## Audience and single job

**Primary audience:** Individuals and families who need immediate or ongoing support — including survivors seeking confidential guidance.

**Single job:** Help a person find the right next step within seconds, especially when they need support, while keeping paths open for donors, volunteers, and partners.

## Palette

The mandatory `#2F2C48` (Midnight Violet) anchors trust and continuity. Lilac (`#E9E5F1`) and warm paper (`#FAFAF8`) create calm surfaces without the generic cream-template look. Sage (`#5D7868`) signals stability and community support. Berry (`#9B3D55`) is reserved for urgent-help moments — the emergency strip and crisis reminders — used as accent, not alarm-red.

## Typography

**Literata** (display) gives headlines a composed, empathetic serif presence without the overused Playfair pairing. **Atkinson Hyperlegible** (body) prioritizes readability under stress, with 17px base size and generous line-height. This pairing is specific to trauma-informed, access-first communication.

## Layout concept

Editorial hero: copy left, actionable "Start here" panel right — borrowed from the reference image's asymmetric grid, without stock photography. Service groups use three editorial columns rather than twelve identical cards. Community story uses a z-pattern (visual left, copy right). Get Involved uses asymmetric three-column composition on violet. FAQ uses numbered accordion (process-appropriate numbering).

## Support Thread (signature element)

A thin SVG line motif connects Reach out → Find support → Move forward. It appears in the hero (linking CTAs to the help panel) and in the three-step intake process. On mobile it becomes a vertical guide with numbered nodes — information is never conveyed by color alone.

## Avoiding generic nonprofit and AI aesthetics

No hero stock photo, no impact statistics band, no testimonial carousel, no pity-based copy, no lime-green charity template, no floating SaaS card grid, no rainbow gradients. Violet is used structurally (header, footer, primary CTAs) rather than decoratively. Copy is specific and action-oriented ("Call for support," "Start client intake") rather than motivational slogans.
